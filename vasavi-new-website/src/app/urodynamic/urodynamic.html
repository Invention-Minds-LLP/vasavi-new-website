Urodynamic Study at Vasavi Hospitals, Bangalore

A urodynamic study is an advanced diagnostic assessment designed to understand how your bladder and urinary tract function during urine storage and emptying. This comprehensive test provides precise information about bladder capacity, pressure, urine flow, and muscle coordination, helping our specialists accurately diagnose the underlying cause of your urinary symptoms.

This evaluation is especially valuable when symptoms are complex, persistent, or haven't improved with initial treatment. By thoroughly assessing the lower urinary tract—including the bladder, urethra, and sphincter muscles—we can distinguish between different conditions that may present similar symptoms and create a targeted treatment plan.

When Is a Urodynamic Study Recommended?

Urinary Leakage or Incontinence

Experiencing unexpected urine leakage during daily activities or stress incontinence

Frequent or Urgent Urination

Sudden, strong urge to urinate or frequent bathroom visits, including nighttime symptoms

Difficulty Starting Urination

Trouble initiating urination or experiencing a weak, interrupted urine stream

Incomplete Bladder Emptying

Persistent sensation that your bladder hasn't fully emptied after urination

Recurrent UTIs

Frequent urinary tract infections that require comprehensive evaluation

Pre-Surgical Assessment

Evaluation before certain urological or incontinence surgical procedures

How Is a Urodynamic Study Performed?

Before the Test

Arrive with a comfortably full bladder. Your medical history and medications will be reviewed. No fasting or anesthesia required.

Catheter Placement

A thin, soft catheter is gently placed into the bladder. Additional sensors may be used to measure pressure accurately.

Bladder Filling Phase

The bladder is slowly filled with sterile fluid while monitoring pressure and sensation. You'll report what you feel during filling.

Voiding Phase

Once full, you'll urinate while we measure flow rate and bladder pressure to assess emptying function.

Additional Measurements

Pelvic floor muscle activity may be assessed. Residual urine is measured after voiding to check complete emptying.

After the Test

Resume normal activities immediately. Drink plenty of fluids. Contact us if you experience fever or persistent discomfort.

Tests Commonly Included in Urodynamic Evaluation

Depending on your specific symptoms and medical history, the urodynamic study may include several specialized tests to provide a comprehensive assessment of your bladder and urinary tract function.

Uroflowmetry

Measures urine flow rate and volume to assess how efficiently the bladder empties

Cystometry

Assesses bladder pressure and capacity during filling to evaluate storage function

Pressure Flow Study

Evaluates bladder and urethral function during urination to identify obstruction

Post-Void Residual

Determines the amount of urine remaining in the bladder after voiding

Electromyography (EMG)

Evaluates pelvic floor muscle coordination during bladder filling and emptying

Each test provides crucial information that helps our urologists create a precise diagnosis and personalized treatment plan tailored to your specific condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is the urodynamic study painful?
Most patients experience only mild discomfort or pressure during the procedure. The test is generally well tolerated, and our team takes every measure to ensure your comfort throughout the examination.
2. How long does the urodynamic study take?
The complete urodynamic study typically takes 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the specific tests required for your condition. It's performed as an outpatient procedure, allowing you to return home the same day.
3. What should I expect after the test?
You can resume normal activities immediately after the test. Some patients may experience mild burning during urination or slight blood in the urine temporarily. We recommend drinking plenty of fluids after the procedure.
4. Do I need to prepare for the urodynamic study?
You may be asked to arrive with a comfortably full bladder. Your doctor will provide specific instructions. Generally, fasting or anesthesia is not required, and you can take your regular medications unless advised otherwise.
5. When will I receive my test results?
Your urologist will review the results with you during a follow-up appointment, usually within a few days. They will discuss the findings in detail and recommend an appropriate treatment plan based on the results.
6. Is urodynamic testing safe?
Yes, urodynamic testing is very safe with minimal risks. Complications are rare but may include temporary discomfort, mild bleeding, or a slight risk of urinary tract infection. Our experienced team follows strict protocols to minimize any risks.
7. Will my insurance cover the urodynamic study?
Most insurance plans cover urodynamic testing when medically necessary. We recommend checking with your insurance provider before the test. Our staff can help with any insurance-related questions.
8. Can I drive myself home after the test?
Yes, you can drive yourself home after the urodynamic study as no sedation or anesthesia is used. The procedure does not affect your ability to drive or perform normal activities.
